# Post structure

The default shape for a LinkedIn post. Each part has one job. The user's voice file can override any of it: if they always go story then lesson with no question at the end, do that.

## The default spine

1. **Hook** (two short lines). Stop the scroll. See `hooks.md`.
2. **The problem, in their words.** Make the reader feel seen. Be specific: "You spend Sunday night fixing the payroll export your provider broke" beats "payroll is painful". Use the exact words the buyer used in the interview.
3. **The story or the steps.** What happened, or what to do. This is where the proof lives: the real story, number or decision from `workspace/stories.md`.
4. **The lesson, in one line.** What the reader should take away. If it needs three lines, the post has two points.
5. **The picture of after.** What changes for the reader if they act on it. Concrete: fewer hours, a closed deal, a calmer month-end. Skip it if the story already shows it.
6. **One ask.** See below.

Short version for skimmers: hook, context in two lines, the steps or the story, the lesson in one line, the ask.

## Shapes by format

**Story and lesson:** hook (the moment) → what was going on → what you did → what happened, with the number → the lesson → how the reader can use it → ask.

**Contrarian take:** hook (the claim) → what most people say → why it's wrong, with the mechanism → what works instead → your proof → where the common view is right (this stops it reading as a strawman) → ask.

**Framework:** hook (the problem it solves) → the moment that made you build it → 3 to 5 steps, one line each → how it played out in the original situation → where to start → ask.

**Customer result:** hook (the result) → where the customer was before, the trigger → what changed → the result with numbers → what other buyers in that spot should check → ask. Only name the customer if the user says it's allowed.

**Objection answered:** hook (the objection in the buyer's words) → why it's a fair worry → what's true → proof → what to ask a vendor (including you) → ask.

## The ask

Pick one. Match it to the funnel stage of the idea.

- **Top of funnel:** a follow, or a specific question that can be answered in one sentence. "What's the one report your CFO asks for that nobody can produce?" works. "Thoughts?" doesn't.
- **Middle of funnel:** a save, a repost, or a reply with their own version.
- **Bottom of funnel:** a DM, a demo link, or "comment X and I'll send you the template".

A sharp closing statement with no question is also fine, and often better than a generic question.

## Length and spacing

- One idea per line or short paragraph. A blank line between them.
- Keep most sentences under about 12 words. Vary the length so it doesn't sound like a metronome.
- Roughly 1,000 to 2,000 characters for most posts. LinkedIn's limit is 3,000.
- Plain words. If a shorter word works, use it.
