# Voice guide

How to capture how someone actually writes and talks, and how to write like them. Used by `onboarding-interview` to build `workspace/voice.md`, and by `write-post` and `review-draft` to apply it.

## Why this matters

A model left alone writes in its own style: smooth, balanced, a bit bland. The only fix is specific rules backed by the person's real lines. "Be warm" does nothing. "Opens with a moment from the week, uses one-line paragraphs, ends on a short dry line, never uses exclamation marks" does.

## What to capture

Build every section from evidence: pasted posts first, then emails or anything else they wrote, then interview answers. Quote real lines. If you don't have evidence for something, leave it out rather than guess.

1. **Who they are in one paragraph.** Role, background, the experience that gives them the right to an opinion.
2. **Sentences.** Short or long? Lots of fragments? First person singular or "we"? Present or past tense for stories?
3. **Paragraphs.** One line each, or small blocks? How much white space?
4. **How they open.** Two or three patterns, each with a real example line.
5. **How they close.** Two or three patterns, each with a real example, and what kind of post each fits.
6. **Transitions.** The phrases they use to move between thoughts ("So.", "Here's what happened next", "Which is when...").
7. **Punctuation and formatting.** Emojis (which, how often), dashes, parentheses, bullets, caps for emphasis, exclamation marks.
8. **Words they use.** Their jargon, their phrases, their way of naming the problem. Note any line they repeat that people associate with them.
9. **Words they never use.** Anything absent from their writing, plus anything they said they hate.
10. **Never sound like.** From the interview: salesy, preachy, corporate, jokey, like everyone else in the industry.
11. **Exceptions to the house rules.** If they use em dashes or emojis all the time, that's their style. Note it here so drafts keep it.

## When there's nothing to paste

Many founders don't post yet. Then use their interview answers, especially the ones they gave quickly and casually. People's spoken answers are closer to their real voice than anything they polish. Ask them to answer one question as if to a friend ("What's the most annoying thing about your industry?") and model the voice on that. Mark `voice.md` as "built from interview, refresh after 5 posts".

## Writing in their voice

- Match their sentence length and paragraph rhythm before anything else. That's most of what people recognise.
- Use their openers and closers, adapted, not pasted.
- Use their words for the problem, the buyer and the product.
- Don't make them sound smarter, cleaner or more polished than they are. If they write rough, keep it rough.
- If a line sounds like it could be from anyone, rewrite it with a detail only they would know.

## Words to avoid by default

Unless their own writing uses them: delve, synergy, game-changer, revolutionize, disrupt, cutting-edge, seamless, robust, holistic, paradigm, ecosystem (as filler), journey (for business), thrilled to announce, humbled, excited to share, in today's fast-paced world, at the end of the day, let that sink in, "Agree?" as a closing line.

Also avoid corporate filler verbs that make a sentence vaguer, and anything a buyer would never say out loud on a call.

## House rules for every draft

- Contractions: "don't", "it's", "we're".
- No em dashes unless their voice file says they use them.
- Sentence case. No title case headings, no all-caps lines.
- No hashtags, or one at most if they always use one.
- No made-up numbers or quotes.
