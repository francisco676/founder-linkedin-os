# LinkedIn basics

What a founder needs to know about how LinkedIn works. Kept to the things that change what you post. LinkedIn doesn't publish how its feed ranks posts, so treat anything below as working guidance, not law.

## How a post spreads

- LinkedIn shows a new post to a slice of your network first. If those people stop, read, comment and save, it shows it to more people, including people who don't follow you.
- Comments count for more than likes, and real comments (a sentence or more) count for more than "Great post".
- When someone your buyer follows comments on your post, your buyer is more likely to see it. That's why who engages matters more than how many.
- The feed favours posts on the topic you're known for. A payroll founder posting about payroll will tend to travel further than the same founder posting about leadership in general. Stay close to your pillars.

## What to do because of that

- **Reply to every comment in the first hour or two.** Block the time when you post. Replies keep the conversation going and show the post to more people.
- **Comment on other people's posts every day.** 10 to 15 minutes, on posts your buyers read. Lead with your own experience, not "Great point". This grows a founder account faster than posting more.
- **Post when your buyer is at work.** Mornings in their time zone, Tuesday to Thursday, is a sensible default. If your buyer is in another time zone, use theirs.
- **Be consistent.** Three posts a week, every week, beats bursts. The plan in `workspace/strategy.md` is built around that.

## Format notes

- **Text posts** are the base. Most founder posts should be text, sometimes with an image.
- **Images:** a real photo or a screenshot of proof. Portrait (4:5) takes more feed space than landscape.
- **Document posts (carousels):** good for frameworks and lists people want to save.
- **Video:** good for trust (product demos, talking through a decision), less good for growing followers.
- **Links:** people worry links hurt distribution. Test it for yourself. If you're unsure, put the link in the first comment and say so in the post.
- **Hashtags:** not needed. LinkedIn reads what the post is about from the words.
- **Length:** the limit is 3,000 characters. About two lines show before "see more" on a phone.

## Profile basics

Before posting a lot, check the profile does its job, because people who like a post click through to it:

- **Headline:** who you help and with what, not just "Founder at X".
- **Banner:** one line on what the company does, and how to get in touch.
- **About:** the problem you solve, who for, one proof point, how to contact you.
- **Featured:** one link to the thing you want people to do (book a demo, read a case study, join a newsletter).

## Measuring

- Judge top of funnel posts by follows, saves and comments from the right people.
- Judge bottom of funnel posts by DMs, demo requests and replies from buyers. They'll get fewer likes. That's expected.
- Once a month, note which 3 posts brought in the best conversations, and tell Claude during `refresh`. More ideas like those go into `ideas.md`.
