# Build strategy

Read first: `workspace/profile.md`, `workspace/stories.md`, `workspace/voice.md`, `knowledge/linkedin-basics.md`. Write `workspace/strategy.md` from `templates/strategy.md`.

## 1. The one-line goal

Write who the posts are for and what should happen after they read them. For example, a founder of a payroll startup might land on: "Get finance leads at 50 to 300 person companies to book a demo before their next payroll provider renewal."

## 2. Pick 3 or 4 content pillars

A pillar is a topic the founder can post about for a year without running dry, and that their buyer cares about. Each pillar must pass three tests:

- **Buyer test:** would the person who signs the contract stop for this?
- **Proof test:** does `stories.md` hold at least two stories, wins or beliefs for it?
- **Only-you test:** could a competitor's founder write the same posts? If yes, sharpen it.

Good pillars usually come from these four places:

1. **The problem**: the trigger and pain from the interview, explained better than the buyer could explain it.
2. **The belief**: the position they hold that competitors don't.
3. **The build**: lessons from building the company (hiring, pricing, mistakes, decisions).
4. **The proof**: what customers achieved and how.

For each pillar write: a name, one line on what it covers, the buyer question it answers, and which stories feed it.

## 3. Set the funnel mix

- **Top of funnel** (attention): broad lessons, stories and opinions that the buyer and people like them share. Gets followers.
- **Middle of funnel** (trust): how you think, frameworks, behind the scenes, teardown of a common mistake. Makes the right people trust you.
- **Bottom of funnel** (action): customer results, how the product solves the trigger, objections answered, a clear ask. Gets demos.

Default mix: 40% top, 40% middle, 20% bottom. Shift it if the interview gives a reason. A founder with almost no followers needs more top. A founder with a big audience and no pipeline needs more bottom. Write the reason in the file.

Bottom of funnel posts get fewer likes and more meetings. Say that in the file so the founder doesn't judge them on likes.

## 4. Cadence

Default: 3 posts a week, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday mornings in the buyer's time zone. If the founder said they can only do less, plan for that. Two posts a week every week beats five one week and none the next.

Add the habit that matters as much as posting: block 30 minutes after each post to reply to every comment, and spend 15 minutes a day leaving real comments on posts their buyers read.

## 5. What to stop posting

List 3 to 5 things, specific to them. Common ones:

- Company announcements with no lesson in them (funding, feature launches, "we're hiring") as standalone posts
- Reposting other people's content with "great read"
- Generic motivation that any founder could post
- Posts about the product that open with the product name
- Topics their buyer doesn't care about, even if they get likes (for example, a B2B founder posting about remote work culture when they sell to CFOs)

If they pasted past posts, name which ones to stop doing and why.

## 6. Finish

Show the pillars and the mix in a short block. Then go straight to the `post-ideas` skill.
